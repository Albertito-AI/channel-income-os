# Channel Income OS: sustainable creative work through better decisions


## Abstract and evidence level

Channel Income OS investigates whether creator exports can support a clearer allocation of attention to rights, anomalies, retention and discovery. The current commercial preview implements local descriptive calculations and a deterministic action queue. This paper defines the measures, explains the implemented priority rules, and proposes evaluation of understanding and decision quality. It distinguishes reproducible arithmetic from causal effectiveness and highlights cases where missing data can produce overconfident recommendations.

This revision was prepared on 15 November 2026 from the exact source revision below. The audit is static: no new software run, creator study or revenue result is reported. Synthetic examples are teaching devices, not customer evidence.

## A decision system, not an income promise

Creators need to decide where to spend scarce effort. A dashboard can show views and revenue while leaving the practical question unanswered: should the next hour improve discovery, repair retention, investigate an anomaly or resolve rights? Channel Income OS translates supplied exports into a local, inspectable queue of possible interventions.

The thesis is that sustainable creative work benefits from disciplined decisions under uncertainty. The aim is not to maximise attention at any cost. It is to help creators, educators and musicians understand the relationship between their catalogue, audience, rights and financial constraints, while keeping their data under their control.

Prepared for Albert Tan Sin Hon with AI assistance on 2 November 2026. This paper describes the inspected commercial preview and proposes future development. It supplies no revenue guarantee, investment recommendation, verified customer result or endorsement by YouTube or Google.

## First principles: observations, scenarios and causes differ

An export records observations over a particular period and scope. It may omit rows, contain adjusted estimates or combine unlike formats. Before interpreting performance, the system must preserve the denominator, dates, currency, units and missing fields. A precise calculation over incomplete rows can still be a misleading account of the channel.

Effective RPM is a descriptive ratio:

$$
\mathrm{RPM}=1000\frac{\text{estimated revenue}}{\text{views}}.
$$

For positive RPM, the corresponding target-view calculation is a scenario based on holding that ratio fixed. It is not a forecast that future views will earn the same amount. Zero, missing or unstable revenue needs an explicit unavailable or caution state rather than an invented positive yield.

Retention ratio, computed from average view duration and video duration, is also a proxy. It hides the shape of the retention curve. A channel-relative percentile helps prioritise within the supplied sample, but it cannot establish why a video performed as it did. Causal claims require a suitable comparison or controlled experiment.

## Fundamental logic of the current engine

The local Python tool reads YouTube Studio CSV exports and optional summary and rights records. It generates a Markdown report, private HTML dashboard, action queue and structured scorecard. It requires no account credentials, uploads or telemetry. If no channel summary is supplied, reconstructed aggregates carry a warning.

The engine distinguishes seven actions: rights hold, revenue audit, retention repair, discovery improvement, scaling a strong performer, packaging test and monitoring. The categories are decision prompts, not commands to publish or spend. Several rules use pooled catalogue quartiles; the packaging rule also has fixed thresholds, and very small or mixed catalogues can make relative thresholds misleading.

A low-view, unusually high-RPM video can become an audit candidate. Explicit adverse rights statuses take precedence over scaling, but the default `unknown` status does not currently trigger a hold. The design goal is to investigate fragile evidence and resolve permissions before promotion; the present implementation does not enforce that goal for every missing or uncertain input.

## Definitions, a worked calculation and a failure case

An **observation window** identifies the period represented by an export. **Effective RPM** is this tool's estimated-revenue-to-views ratio, not an automatic reproduction of every YouTube metric. A **cohort** is the set of rows against which thresholds are computed. An **action code** is a priority prompt generated by a rule, not a causal finding or a permission to scale.

For a fictional period with 10,000 views and USD 20 estimated revenue, effective RPM is USD 2 per thousand views. Holding that ratio fixed, a USD 100 target corresponds to 50,000 views. Doubling the desired amount doubles that scenario's required views; it does not make those views attainable or the ratio stable. A missing or zero revenue value makes the current summary RPM nonpositive and the target-view field unavailable. The importer does not consistently preserve a distinct missing-data state: many absent or malformed numeric fields default to zero. A reader must therefore inspect the export, not equate every zero with observed absence.

The retention property in [src/channel_income_os/models.py](https://github.com/Albertito-AI/channel-income-os/blob/e76db00030b3dbd2f097c1e33af18134cf19c982/src/channel_income_os/models.py) calculates average duration divided by video duration, then clamps the result to 0–1.5; nonpositive duration becomes zero. Percentiles in [src/channel_income_os/utils.py](https://github.com/Albertito-AI/channel-income-os/blob/e76db00030b3dbd2f097c1e33af18134cf19c982/src/channel_income_os/utils.py) use linear interpolation over sorted values. The classification cohort pools the supplied rows, while separate learning/other summaries do not create separate classification thresholds.

The rules have consequential exceptions. Rights hold is triggered by the explicit statuses `hold`, `uncleared`, `disputed` and `blocked`; default `unknown` does not trigger it. The low-sample revenue audit uses a default view count below 25 and the positive-revenue cohort's 95th-percentile RPM. The later packaging rule uses fixed CTR below 2% with at least 1,000 impressions, after higher-priority rules. A zero CTR does not enter that branch because the condition also tests its truth value. Thus the engine is partly relative and partly fixed, rather than universally cohort-derived.

A static counterexample makes the limitation concrete. Supply a one-video catalogue with 100 views, zero revenue, missing/nonpositive duration and `unknown` rights. View quartiles both equal 100; the empty retention cohort supplies zero thresholds; the video retention proxy is zero. The current branch order can label it `IMPROVE_DISCOVERY`, despite having no positive retention measurement. This follows from [src/channel_income_os/analysis.py](https://github.com/Albertito-AI/channel-income-os/blob/e76db00030b3dbd2f097c1e33af18134cf19c982/src/channel_income_os/analysis.py), [src/channel_income_os/models.py](https://github.com/Albertito-AI/channel-income-os/blob/e76db00030b3dbd2f097c1e33af18134cf19c982/src/channel_income_os/models.py) and [src/channel_income_os/utils.py](https://github.com/Albertito-AI/channel-income-os/blob/e76db00030b3dbd2f097c1e33af18134cf19c982/src/channel_income_os/utils.py); it was not executed during this revision. It is a reason to add explicit missingness and minimum-cohort tests, not to describe the existing queue as validated advice.

## Related work and metric compatibility

[YouTube Help, “Understand ad revenue analytics”](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314357/), accessed 15 November 2026, distinguishes RPM from advertiser CPM and distinguishes Shorts engaged-view denominators from video views. It also identifies revenue categories that RPM does not include. Consequently, this tool's custom effective RPM should be compared with Studio only after aligning revenue scope, format, denominator, currency and dates. The help page is platform documentation, not evidence of this tool's accuracy or a stable future earnings rate.

[Kohavi, Longbotham, Sommerfield and Henne, “Controlled experiments on the web: survey and practical guide”, Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery 18, 140–181 (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1) explains why controlled experiments, appropriate sample sizes and clear criteria matter for causal claims. The publication record and abstract were inspected; its experiments were not reproduced here. A creator's before/after title change is not automatically randomised, and the current application provides no experiment-assignment engine or causal estimator. Its queue generates hypotheses for a separately designed evaluation.

## A concrete use case

Imagine a small educational channel has one highly watched short video and several longer lessons with fewer views but stronger retention. The creator could easily focus on the largest number. A useful report asks whether format, exposure, period and audience make the rows comparable, and whether the less-discovered lessons deserve a packaging experiment.

The proposed experiment should have a dated change, a stated objective and a comparison window. Record what happened without attributing every difference to the intervention. Seasonality, topic demand and distribution can change at the same time. If rights for a soundtrack are unresolved, that video should remain on hold regardless of its engagement potential.

The software's value is the quality of this decision process. More recommended actions are not inherently better. A short, well-supported queue may be more useful than an elaborate report that overwhelms a creator.

## Social purpose and ethical limits

The immediate aim is operational clarity for people whose creative work must also be financially sustainable. The larger hope is that useful education, music and public-interest communication can survive without forcing creators into deceptive growth practices or surrendering private analytics to unnecessary intermediaries.

Financial sustainability and public benefit can conflict. A strategy that increases revenue by misleading viewers, exploiting attention or infringing rights would not satisfy this project's ideal direction. The design should favour honest packaging, relevant audience relationships, rights clarity and experiments that respect viewers.

Affordability and licensing also require precision. The source is inspectable under PolyForm Noncommercial, while commercial use requires separate terms. Public availability is not a conventional open-source grant. Future educational access or sponsorship arrangements are possibilities for the owner to decide, not permissions created by this paper.

## Ideal progression and wishlist

| Stage | Capability | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reliable single-window analysis | Faithful imports, clear missingness and reproducible reports | Diverse permitted export fixtures and independent reproduction |
| Longitudinal decisions | Comparable windows and dated interventions | Explicit periods, stable cohorts, adjustments and uncertainty warnings |
| Better experiments | Separate hypotheses from observed changes | Predeclared outcomes, appropriate controls and retained unsuccessful tests |
| Responsible agency workflow | Multiple channels and client-safe outputs | Access boundaries, consent, retention and leakage checks |
| Stable operating contract | Versioned inputs, scorecards and migrations | Compatibility tests, accessibility review and support evidence |

The wishlist includes format-aware comparisons, cohort preservation, robust revenue reconciliation, clearer rights evidence, accessibility, configurable goals and transparent prioritisation. Goals should allow educational quality, audience trust and creative sustainability alongside revenue. Automated recommendations should remain explainable and overridable.

Optional integrations should be justified by an actual workflow need. A direct API or cloud service would introduce new credential, permission and privacy responsibilities; it should not silently replace the useful credential-free route. Automatic promotional or purchasing actions are outside the current product.

## Measuring whether the project helps

Evaluate whether creators understand the report, identify a defensible next action and avoid misleading extrapolation. Track import errors, missing data, incorrect classifications, rights misunderstandings and time spent checking recommendations. In longitudinal work, distinguish the effect of using the tool from unrelated audience or platform changes.

Income claims need actual, attributable evidence and uncertainty. A synthetic demonstration can establish arithmetic and output generation; it cannot establish willingness to pay, customer success or earnings. The project should revise thresholds when they generate poor decisions rather than preserving them for consistency's sake.

The best case is a calm operating discipline for creative work: people understand their constraints, choose a small number of meaningful experiments, learn from failure and retain ownership of their evidence. Better judgement is the durable product; a projected income number is only one input.

## Evaluation design and teaching prompts

Begin with import fidelity and interpretation before measuring earnings. Create authorised or synthetic fixtures that distinguish zero from missing, combine incomparable formats, contain tied quartiles, include a single row and vary rights status. Have reviewers record their expected classification before inspecting output. Measure false assurance, explanation accuracy, time to a defensible action and whether rights uncertainty is noticed. Proposed code fixes need their own regression evidence; documenting a failure case does not repair it.

For later experiments, predeclare the target outcome and observation period, record audience and format changes, retain unsuccessful interventions, and use a suitable control where available. Treat uncontrolled before/after differences as observational. To assess the tool itself, compare decision quality and effort with ordinary Studio review rather than attributing all channel growth to its recommendations.

Teaching questions are concrete: why does USD 2 RPM imply a 50,000-view scenario for USD 100 but no forecast; how can a zero proxy enter a discovery category; and why can `unknown` rights require human action even without a `RIGHTS_HOLD` code? A learner should be able to distinguish an arithmetic error, an unsuitable metric and an unsupported causal conclusion.
